How it works
The plane is a 5 g body pulled by gravity, lift and drag. Lift and drag depend on speed squared and on the angle of attack, the angle between the wing and the air it moves through. A pitching moment turns the nose back toward its trimmed angle, so a fast throw climbs, slows, stalls, dips and swoops. Paper is floppy: as speed rises the wings bend back, so lift levels off at a few g instead of growing without limit. That is why hard throws fly flat and fast rather than looping.

Values are estimates from a simple model of a 5 g paper plane, not a calibrated wind-tunnel result.
